From variability to radius

Black hole images derived directly from the X-ray power spectrum under the simplified assumption that frequencies correspond to viscous timescales, which map to radii as:

r(ν) = rg [α (H/R)² / (ν tg)]2/3

tg = GM/c³

The dotted line marks 2 RG.

Stochastic azimuthal structure has been added for aesthetic reasons, and (unlike the radii of emission) is not inferred from the data.

Rotation has similarly been added but is purely illustrative and set at 0.01% of the local Keplerian rate.

Sound and interpretation

Sound is continuous random noise shaped by the measured power spectrum, with frequencies shifted upward by 64: a 4–8 Hz QPO becomes a narrow noise peak at 256–512 Hz. There is no separate QPO tone or boost. Power follows max(P − 2, 0), using one fixed gain. Below 0.25 Hz, the average excess power of the first four bins is extrapolated flat toward zero frequency; this is an assumption about unmeasured variability. The low-frequency attenuation control gently reduces the broad continuum below 2 Hz (full attenuation below 0.5 Hz; frequencies quoted before the 64× shift). Set it to 0 dB to hear the unweighted spectrum. During gaps the spectral shape holds, while fresh random noise continues. Audio responds over roughly 0.35 seconds as synthesis blocks overlap. Sound is muted when paused.

Disc brightness uses fixed logarithmic scaling, frequency smoothing, and a faint data-present glow. Blue rings illustrate the inferred launch times; their size and speed do not measure jet propagation. Ejection dates carry published uncertainties, shown as shaded bands.
